# Introduction of Microprocessor

A microprocessor is an integrated circuit that serves as the central processing unit (CPU), acting as the "brain" of a digital system by handling all calculations and decisions. Its fundamental process is simple: it receives input, processes it using math and logic operations, and produces an output.

**Microprocessor Evolution**
Microprocessors have historically evolved based on the number of bits (binary digits) they can process at once. Higher bit capacity directly translates to faster, more powerful computing.

| Era & Architecture | Notable Examples | Technical Capabilities | Real-World Applications |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4-bit** (Early 1970s) | Intel 4004 (1971) | Processed 4 bits at a time; very slow with limited memory. | Calculators, traffic lights, and simple gadgets. |
| **8-bit** (Late 1970s) | Intel 8085 (1976) | Handled 8 bits at once; supported up to 64KB of memory. | Early computers, arcade games, and embedded systems. |
| **16-bit** (1980s) | Intel 8086 (1978) | Processed 16 bits per operation; accessed 1MB+ memory. | Enabled early operating systems (like MS-DOS) and assembly programming. |
| **32-bit** (1990s–2000s) | Intel 80386, Pentium | Processed 32 bits; handled up to 4GB of RAM. | Powered Windows 95, graphical apps, desktops, and laptops. |
| **64-bit** (2000s–Present) | Intel Core i5, AMD Ryzen | Processes 64 bits; accesses billions of memory addresses. | Runs AI, 3D gaming, video editing, and virtual machines. |
| **Multi-Core** (Modern) | Dual-core, Quad-core, Octa-core | Integrates multiple CPUs (cores) into a single chip for parallel processing. | Boosts speed and multitasking efficiency (e.g., gaming while streaming). |
